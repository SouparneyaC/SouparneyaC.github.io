My research interests center on how information gets sourced, priced, and validated in financial markets — treating price as an imperfect aggregator of dispersed information, in the tradition of Grossman & Stiglitz. I've worked on common-ownership index replication and 13F disclosure-timing effects in institutional holdings, crowd-belief compression in a live scored forecasting competition, and statistical validation discipline for small-sample model selection. Across all of it, the throughline is the same question: how do you measure the marginal value of an information source, in a way that's quantitative, testable, and survives out-of-sample scrutiny — not just in one domain, but as a general method.
