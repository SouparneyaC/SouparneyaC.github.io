# FIN330 / FIN570 — Course Assistant

> the same fitted equation, USRevenue = 53.70 − 0.50·Budget + 3.24·Opening − 0.0012·Theaters, assigned under one course number to undergraduates and under another to MBA students, the same semester.

## Overview

Course Assistant, Spring 2026, for FIN330, Quantitative Methods, an undergraduate statistics-for-finance course taught by Professor Lan Zhang. The syllabus itself lists the role directly: "Course Assistant: Souparneya Chakrabarti." The same semester's material was also assigned to FIN570, the MBA-level section of substantially the same course; no separate FIN570 syllabus survives, but its own assignment documents, headed "FIN570 Spring 2026," carry the identical regression problems FIN330's own solved homework works through, confirmed directly against both sets of files.

This role followed straight out of the [FIN442 TA position](/teaching/fin442/): asked by the department which other Finance courses would fit, I made the case for FIN330 by name, citing ECON270 as materially equivalent preparation and STAT381, STAT382, and STAT481 as more advanced statistical grounding, despite not having taken FIN330 itself.

## What the course covered

FIN330's own course description frames it directly: statistical inference for financial markets, starting from financial data, estimating key quantities, and using that to test assumptions or relationships rather than assume them. Grading splits six ways: participation 15 percent, homework 15 percent, quizzes 20 percent, two midterms worth 15 percent each, and a final worth 20 percent, six homework assignments total with the lowest dropped.

Two assignments, shared verbatim with FIN570, show what that looks like in practice. One works with tuition data from 33 public universities, plotting 2000 tuition against 2008 tuition, fitting a simple linear regression, checking the residuals for normality, and running a hypothesis test on the slope. The other works with box-office data on 40 movies from Nash Information Services, fitting a multiple regression of total U.S. revenue against a movie's budget, its opening-weekend revenue, and how many theaters it opened in, landing on a fitted equation, revenue rising with opening-weekend performance, falling slightly with a higher budget once the other two predictors are held fixed.

## Where this shows up elsewhere

Every method in these two assignments, simple and multiple linear regression, residual diagnostics, hypothesis testing on a coefficient, is the same material already covered on this site as a student in [STAT381](/coursework/stat381/), [STAT382](/coursework/stat382/), and [STAT481](/coursework/stat481/). Teaching it to an MBA section in FIN570 is the same content taught back, at the graduate level, a few semesters after first learning it as an undergraduate.
