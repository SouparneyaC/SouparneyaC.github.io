# MATH330 — Abstract Algebra I

> the exam's only permitted aid is a single sheet of paper, handwritten only; printing out a photograph of handwritten notes is explicitly against the rules.

## Overview

MATH330, Abstract Algebra I, Fall 2026, taught by Greg Taylor, the same instructor as [STATS401](/coursework/stats401/). Prerequisite: a C or better in MATH215. The syllabus's own description is direct: three fundamental algebraic structures, groups, rings, and fields, with an emphasis on proofs and examples, using Gallian's *Contemporary Abstract Algebra*. This entry is built entirely from the real syllabus; the course begins August 24, 2026, and no graded work exists yet.

## What the course covers

The syllabus's own structure follows its three named objects in order. Groups come first, six weeks moving from the basic definition through finite subgroups, cyclic groups, permutation groups, isomorphisms, and cosets and Lagrange's Theorem, closed out by the first midterm. Rings take over next: external direct products, normal subgroups and factor groups, group homomorphisms, then a shift into ring theory proper, integral domains, ideals and factor rings, ring homomorphisms, polynomial rings, and factorization, closed out by the second midterm on divisibility. The final stretch covers fields: extension fields, algebraic extensions, and finite fields, the structures group and ring theory build toward.

Grading: homework 20 percent (the lowest two dropped), quizzes 10 percent (the lowest two dropped, starting week three), two 50-minute midterms at 20 percent each, and a cumulative two-hour final worth 30 percent. Each of the three exams allows exactly one aid, a single handwritten sheet of paper, one side only, no printed reproductions of handwritten notes permitted; the weekly quizzes allow none.
