# MATH215 — Introduction to Advanced Mathematics

> the same syllabus that spends five weeks on worksheets before a single formal proof gets written calls its own back half "a preview of a standard proof-based course," not the real thing yet.

## Overview

MATH215, Introduction to Advanced Mathematics, Fall 2025, taught by Philip Engel, in-person, no textbook required. The syllabus's own catalog description: an introduction to the methods of proof used across mathematics. Grading: worksheets 30 percent (the lowest four dropped), two in-class midterms at 20 percent each, and a comprehensive final worth 30 percent.

## What the course covered

The first five weeks build vocabulary before any proof-writing starts: set theory, subsets, set operations, indexed collections, partitions, Cartesian products, and the logic of statements, open and quantified statements, negation, conjunction and disjunction, implications and biconditionals, worked through fourteen worksheets before the first midterm.

The next four weeks turn that vocabulary into actual proof-writing: direct proof, proof by contrapositive, proof by cases, proof by contradiction, existence and uniqueness proofs, and mathematical induction, practiced mostly against elementary number theory, divisibility, greatest common divisor, relatively prime integers, prime decomposition, twenty more worksheets by the second midterm.

The final five weeks shift from worksheet-driven practice to lecture-style presentation, the syllabus's own description of it as a preview of a standard proof-based course, not the real thing yet. Equivalence relations, congruence modulo \(d\), and the different flavors of function, injective, surjective, bijective, through composition, inverses, and cardinality, carry the comprehensive final's own emphasis. Thirty-four numbered worksheets were assigned across the full semester.

## What I actually built

Worksheet 19 asks for a direct proof by cases: for any integer \(a\), show that either \(6 \nmid (5a-1)\) or \(9 \nmid (2a+7)\). The first midterm's own practice material worked through power sets, computing \(P(C)\) for a two-element set, set differences, and building a four-part partition of the real numbers with at least one finite piece. The final's practice set included two induction proofs, one showing \(5 \mid (2\cdot 4^n + 3\cdot 9^n)\) for every nonnegative integer \(n\), the other a telescoping product identity. My own Midterm 1 submission survives as a handwritten scan, illegible to text extraction but real, one of very few handwritten rather than typed submissions recovered anywhere in this coursework record.

## Performance

The course builds correction directly into the grade: a missed midterm problem can be resubmitted, corrected, to recover up to a third of the lost credit. A surviving corrections worksheet is keyed to specific missed problems, partitioning a set, computing \(P(A) \cup P(B)\) and its cardinality, set differences \(A \setminus B\) and \(B \setminus A\), not a generic redo.
